Healthcare & clinics

Connect collections, clinic activity and cash.

Multiple payment channels, practitioners and locations can make revenue difficult to reconcile. Management needs a clear view of service income, refunds, direct costs and collection timing without bringing patient details into routine finance reporting.

Collection reconciliation

Trace service income to the books.

  • Payment-processor clearing and refund schedules
  • Direct versus clinic-channel reporting structures
  • Revenue cut-off and exception reporting

Clinic economics

Compare activity and financial performance.

  • Location and service-line contribution
  • Practitioner and support-cost scenarios
  • Budgets reflecting opening hours and capacity

Cash and oversight

Plan for commitments before they fall due.

  • Cash forecasts for payroll, rent and equipment
  • Monthly packs for owners and finance teams
  • Reconciliation responsibilities and close checklists

Measures that support decisions

Revenue by channel, collection lag, location contribution, payroll ratios and capacity assumptions. Clinical outcomes and patient advice are outside the finance scope.

How the work starts

Begin with de-identified financial exports, processor statements and location codes. We agree the revenue mapping, identify unsettled balances and build a pack that separates trading performance from cash timing.

We agree the initial diagnostic, reconcile the starting information, develop the reporting or model, and walk through findings and update responsibilities. Ongoing support follows only where agreed.

The scope is built around your records and financial questions. Sector examples describe reporting needs, rather than a claim of a named C&A client engagement.

Practical questions

Do you need patient records?

Routine finance work should use de-identified or aggregated data. Access is limited to the information required for the agreed financial task.

Can you compare several clinics?

Yes, where consistent location codes and cost allocations are available. Shared costs and collection differences are explained before comparing margins.
